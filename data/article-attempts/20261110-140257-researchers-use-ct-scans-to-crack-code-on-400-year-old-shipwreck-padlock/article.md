## Advanced Scans Solve a Centuries-Old Mystery

Researchers and museum experts have solved a 400-year-old puzzle after cracking the combination to a brass padlock recovered from a historic shipwreck in the UK. The 16th-century artifact had remained firmly secured since it was buried on a beach in Wales, guarding its secret internal mechanism for more than four centuries.

To overcome the challenge without damaging the delicate historical object, museum experts collaborated with specialists from Cardiff University. The research team turned to non-invasive CT scanning technology to look inside the lock's housing. By generating detailed structural images of the interior mechanism, the scans revealed the precise letter combination required to open it: ANCE.

Researchers involved in the project noted that CT technology is typically applied to examine biological structures and bones, making the study of a 400-year-old padlock an unprecedented departure from their normal work. However, the non-destructive imaging proved completely successful, allowing scientists to pinpoint the hidden alignment and unlock the historic piece.

## Discovery of the Ann Francis Treasure Hoard

The brass combination lock represents a single component of the broader Ann Francis treasure hoard. This collection of historical artifacts was gradually brought to light over a 21-year timeframe between 1996 and 2017 by metal detectorist Peter Hughes. Among the items recovered alongside the padlock was a hoard of silver coins.

Upon initial discovery of the secured lock, Hughes reported being immediately astonished by its remarkable state of preservation and the quality of its craftsmanship. Despite spending centuries submerged on a beach, the lock retained its structural integrity, preserving the internal components that ultimately allowed CT scans to decipher the code.

Following its recovery and technical analysis, the padlock, along with other artifacts from the Ann Francis treasure hoard, has been housed at the Museum of Wales.

## Historical Significance and Preservation Success

According to Alastair Willis, Senior Curator for Numismatics and the Welsh Economy at Amgueddfa Cymru, combination locks from the Elizabethan era are known within Britain. However, Willis highlighted that this specific specimen is exceptionally rare because of its extraordinary condition and its direct association with a dated shipwreck.

That historical context made the secured lock a unique physical finding and turned its hidden four-letter code into a compelling mystery for researchers. The successful application of modern medical scanning technology highlights how advanced non-invasive methods can resolve centuries-old historical questions without endangering fragile ancient objects.

## What the Evidence Shows

The non-destructive CT scan revealed the four-letter code ANCE, successfully unlocking a 16th-century brass padlock from the Ann Francis treasure hoard without structural damage. Recovered over a 21-year period by metal detectorist Peter Hughes, the exceptionally preserved Elizabethan artifact is now housed at the Museum of Wales.
