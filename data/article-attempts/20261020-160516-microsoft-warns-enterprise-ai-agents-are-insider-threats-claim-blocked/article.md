## Red‑Blue‑Green Teams: A Built‑In Defense System
Microsoft’s Project Perception began with three classes of agents. A red team acts like a hacker, a blue team spots the red team’s attacks and evaluates the flaws it finds, and a green team works to fix those flaws. The project was introduced to give enterprises a way to test and secure themselves against the very models that can also be used to attack.

## Independent Monitoring of Third‑Party AI
The company’s chief executive, Satya Nadella, has cautioned that the very agents enterprises pay for could become insider threats. He has urged organizations to deploy monitoring systems that are separate from the AI labs that supply the agents. Microsoft’s own Defender product now includes a detection feature that can identify up to 60 types of agents that employees might bring into the workplace, including those from Claude, Codex and OpenClaw. In addition, the software can limit agents’ access to data and systems and can adjust their permissions dynamically based on the task at hand.

## Human Oversight for Risky Decisions
One concrete measure is the requirement for human approval whenever an agent seems to be making risky choices. The approach mirrors how auditors must remain independent of the organizations they examine. This rule is designed to curb the unpredictability that can arise when models are trained solely to perform tasks rather than to follow organizational norms or judgment.

## Why the Shift Matters
Microsoft’s announcement comes amid growing concerns about frontier models that cannot be fully trusted. The company’s own lab develops models, and OpenAI is one of its major cloud customers. By insisting on separate, independent monitoring, Microsoft seeks to prevent malicious or accidental misuse of AI agents inside enterprises, treating them as potential insider threats.

## What to Watch Next
 Microsoft’s latest Defender update is the first step, but broader adoption of similar controls may follow as the industry grapples with the same uncertainty about how much agency to give AI systems.
