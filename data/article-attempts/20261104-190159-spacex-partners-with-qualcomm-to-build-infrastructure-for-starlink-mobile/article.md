SpaceX has entered into a deal with chipmaker Qualcomm to supply key network technology for Starlink Mobile, establishing a direct terrestrial challenge to established carriers like Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile. The announcement caused shares of U.S. carriers to decline.

## Network Infrastructure and Expansion

Under the multiyear contract, Qualcomm will provide its Dragonwing cellular infrastructure hardware and software for base stations. Qualcomm stated on Wednesday that this terrestrial network will complement Starlink's satellite coverage, with a planned rollout starting next year. 

Durga Malladi, an executive vice president at Qualcomm, noted in a statement that the collaboration aims to build cellular infrastructure in the United States, advancing current 5G tech while laying a foundation for AI-native 6G. Qualcomm has targeted carriers and infrastructure providers since expanding into 5G infrastructure in 2020. SpaceX represents one of Qualcomm's highest-profile infrastructure partners, following previous deals with Verizon, Dish, and Europe's Vodafone. Although Qualcomm is widely recognized for modems and radios inside devices, it also produces technology for network base stations.

While SpaceX intends to use Qualcomm's technology to build tens of thousands of new cell sites, it has not named cell tower partners or specified how many new towers will be required. SpaceX did not respond to requests for additional information.

## Strategic Growth and Market Impact

Starlink remains a primary revenue driver for SpaceX, generating 36% of its overall revenue and 85% of its operating profit in the third quarter. Financial terms of the Qualcomm agreement were not disclosed.

To expand its mobile footprint, SpaceX is acquiring spectrum and forming partnerships. Earlier, the Washington Post reported that SpaceX is in talks to acquire EchoStar's Boost Mobile.

## What the Evidence Shows

SpaceX is partnering with Qualcomm to build a U.S. ground network using Dragonwing hardware and software for Starlink Mobile. The planned rollout begins next year to complement satellite services, though details regarding tower partners remain unannounced. Starlink continues to drive the majority of SpaceX's operating profit as the company expands its reach in mobile communications.
