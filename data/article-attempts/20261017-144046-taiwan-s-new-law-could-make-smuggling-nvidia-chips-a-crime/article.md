## New Taiwanese Regulation Targets AI‑Chip Smuggling
Taiwan announced a plan to criminalize the shipment of AI chips to China. The new rule would make such transfers a crime under the Foreign Trade Act, closing a legal loophole that has kept the activity technically legal. The change could reduce gray‑market demand that never appeared in official sales figures and lower regulatory risk for companies involved.

## China’s Potential Approval of Nvidia RTX PRO 5500
The Chinese government has signaled that it could allow domestic firms such as ByteDance and Alibaba to purchase Nvidia’s RTX PRO 5500, a chip built for high‑end professional computers. Industry executives expect the chip to sidestep existing U.S. export restrictions. Nvidia officials said that U.S. firms remain constrained by outdated export controls covering older gaming products and by China’s limits on U.S. imports. Alibaba and ByteDance have not yet commented.

## Implications for U.S. and Taiwanese Companies
The Taiwanese move would replace the existing controlled‑goods list with a blanket restriction covering the entire Chinese market. It would make shipments of AI chips a crime, potentially tightening enforcement against smuggling networks. The change is part of a broader effort to align Taiwan’s trade controls with U.S. policy, following a trade agreement signed in January that reduced U.S. tariffs on Taiwanese goods. A legislative amendment adding a mainland China semiconductor clause is still pending.

## What to Watch
The new law represents a significant tightening of the regulatory net around AI‑chip transfers to China. Companies that have relied on the current legal gap may need to adjust their supply chains. The outcome will depend on how quickly the legislation is finalized and how firms respond to the increased legal risk. Observers should monitor the passage of the amendment and any enforcement actions that follow.

## What the Evidence Shows
China may permit domestic firms to buy Nvidia’s RTX PRO 5500, while Taiwan moves to criminalize AI‑chip shipments to China, tightening a legal gap that has allowed major semiconductor firms to operate with lower regulatory risk.
