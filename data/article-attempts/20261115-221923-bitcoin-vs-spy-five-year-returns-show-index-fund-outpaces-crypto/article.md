## Five‑Year Performance Head‑to‑Head
Bitcoin’s price has risen 7% over the last five years, while the SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY) gained 61% on price alone. That translates to a $10,000 investment growing to $10,740 in Bitcoin versus $16,144 in SPY. The index fund’s higher return reflects a broader market rally that Bitcoin has not matched.

## Total Return and Dividends
Bitcoin pays no dividends, so its total return relies entirely on price appreciation. SPY distributes dividends from its underlying companies, and when those dividends are reinvested, the five‑year total return reaches about 72.6%. That would turn a $10,000 stake into roughly $17,260, outperforming Bitcoin’s price‑only gain.

## Implications for Long‑Term Investors
Because Bitcoin’s price movements are steep, a loss can take many years to recover. For retirees who need regular income, a cash payout from dividends can be critical. A significant drawdown in Bitcoin could leave a retiree unable to replace lost gains, a risk that planners term sequence‑of‑returns risk.

## Market Context
While Bitcoin is often praised for its ability to generate wealth, the matched five‑year numbers favor a plain index fund by a wide margin. Over the same period, SPY’s price climbed from $467.27 on November 12, 2021 to $754.37 on November 13, 2026, a 61.44% gain. Over ten years, SPY’s price gain reaches 245.6%.

## What the Evidence Shows
The evidence demonstrates that, over the last five years, SPY’s price and total return significantly outperformed Bitcoin’s price alone. For investors prioritizing steady gains and income, the index fund presents a more reliable option than the highly volatile cryptocurrency. However, investors should weigh their own risk tolerance and income needs before choosing between the two.
