## Independent Committees Formed to Review Tie-Up

SpaceX and Tesla have taken preliminary steps to explore a potential combination by establishing separate special committees of independent directors. CEO Elon Musk shared the update during SpaceX's third-quarter earnings call on Tuesday, noting that the move follows heightened market speculation and direct requests from shareholders. Musk explained that because he serves as the chief executive of both companies, securities regulations create a conflict of interest that prevents him from sitting on either committee or participating directly in the negotiations. Addressing analysts, he described the situation as an odd situation where two distinct groups are conducting talks without his presence, adding that even answering questions about the proposed deal felt like navigating a sensitive path.

While Musk expressed strong personal support for the idea, calling a potential merger a good idea and pointing to merit in bringing the companies together during an era of rapid technological advancement, he cautioned against assuming an agreement is guaranteed. He explicitly advised against treating a deal as an inevitable outcome. Furthermore, any transaction would face substantial structural and regulatory hurdles, particularly for Tesla, which is scheduled to hold its annual shareholder meeting on December 3. For Tesla to approve such a transaction, it would require a vote of approval from 50 percent of all outstanding shares, rather than a simple majority of votes cast, which Musk identified as a high hurdle to clear.

## Synergies and Strategic Trade-Offs

Discussion surrounding a possible combination centers on key operational and technical overlaps between the two entities. Musk highlighted several potential areas of cooperation, including superintelligence software, chip design, robotics, mass-to-orbit capabilities tied to the Starlink satellite network, and the joint Tesla-SpaceX Terafab semiconductor project. Broadly, most Wall Street analysts and legal experts anticipate that if a transaction occurs, SpaceX would acquire Tesla or absorb the electric vehicle maker through a stock swap, rather than Tesla buying SpaceX.

However, a deal presents significant financial and operational trade-offs for both organizations. If Tesla were to acquire SpaceX directly, it would need to issue millions of new stock shares. For existing Tesla shareholders, this dilution would result in owning a smaller percentage of a much larger combined entity. Additionally, SpaceX's ambitious launch goals require funding of tens of billions of dollars each year. Diverting or adding this scale of capital expenditure could potentially drain resources that Tesla otherwise needs to construct new vehicle assembly plants and battery manufacturing facilities.

## Financial Context and Technical Integration

The announcement comes alongside strong financial performance from SpaceX during its third quarter. The company reported revenue of $14.58 billion, topping Wall Street expectations of $13 billion. Earnings per share reached $0.35, significantly beating the forecasted $0.13. SpaceX's CFO, Johnsen, noted that the company closed the quarter with a substantial order backlog totaling $63 billion as of September 30. The quarterly beat was primarily driven by a surge in superintelligence segment revenue, which doubled part of its top line by renting out computing capacity to outside artificial intelligence firms and surpassed Starlink connectivity revenue for the first time.

Corporate ties between Musk's ventures have already been expanding prior to these formal merger talks. In January, Tesla announced a $2 billion investment to acquire shares in an AI startup subsidiary of SpaceX. The following month, in February, SpaceX acquired xAI, which Musk subsequently renamed SpaceXSI. Operational expansion continues as well, with Musk outlining plans to secure an incremental 20 gigawatts of power and cooling capacity by the end of next year, alongside a short-term strategy for SpaceX to rent out AI data centers.

## What the Evidence Shows

SpaceX and Tesla have formed independent special committees to evaluate a potential merger, though CEO Elon Musk emphasizes that a deal is not certain and faces significant shareholder voting thresholds. Potential technical synergies span AI software, semiconductors, and robotics, but a deal involves strategic financial trade-offs regarding capital spending and share issuance.
