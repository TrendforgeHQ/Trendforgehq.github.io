## Background of the Approval
On October 2, 2026, the U.S.The products, a 3× Bitcoin fund (ticker BITH) and a 3× Ether fund (ticker ETHK), are designed to provide triple the daily price action of their underlying cryptocurrencies.The approval also includes 3× funds for gold, silver, crude oil, and natural gas.

## How the 3× Funds Operate
The Bitcoin and Ether funds will use futures contracts rather than holding the physical coins. Their prospectuses state that the Bitcoin fund will charge a 1.85 % management fee annually. To break even, the Bitcoin fund must earn 1.98 % per year, and the Ether fund must earn 2.78 % per year.

Leveraged ETFs aim to provide leveraged exposure on a daily basis, but their performance over longer periods can diverge sharply from the underlying asset because of volatility decay.

On September 21, Bitcoin traded between $80,837 and $87,397, an 8 % range in a single day.

## Comparison to Direct Holdings
Investing directly in Bitcoin or Ether, or through spot ETFs, costs far less in fees and avoids the downside volatility that leverage can introduce. The prospectus’s break‑even thresholds suggest that even moderate returns would not cover the costs of the leveraged funds.

The Motley Fool’s analyst team does not include Bitcoin among its top ten recommended stocks, while it does hold positions in both Bitcoin and Ether. This stance highlights a preference for direct exposure over leveraged alternatives.

## Potential Risks and Uncertainties
Because the funds rely on futures contracts, they may be more sensitive to roll‑over costs and liquidity conditions than spot‑based products. The volatility decay phenomenon means that prolonged market swings could erode the fund’s value even if the underlying asset eventually recovers.

The funds’ management fees and the higher break‑even rates add another layer of cost.

## What to Watch Going Forward

- Market conditions such as Bitcoin’s price volatility and the overall liquidity of futures markets will influence how the funds perform.

## Takeaway
The SEC’s approval marks the first triple‑leveraged Bitcoin and Ether ETFs in the United States, offering a new way to bet on cryptocurrency price movements. However, the high fees, the necessity of earning above‑average returns to break even, and the risk of volatility decay suggest that these products may not be suitable for most investors. Buying Bitcoin or Ether directly—or via spot ETFs—remains a cheaper and less risky alternative for those seeking exposure to the underlying assets.

What the Evidence Shows:
- The SEC approved triple‑leveraged Bitcoin and Ether ETFs in October 2026.
- The funds will use futures contracts and charge 1.85 % annual fees, with higher yearly return thresholds for break‑even.
- Volatility decay can lead to fund losses even when the underlying asset gains.
