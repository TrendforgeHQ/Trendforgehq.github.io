## U.S. chips are the unseen backbone of Chinese AI
The Commerce Department has recently issued a rule to close a loophole that allowed foreign entities to use U.S. chips for AI inference. Despite this move, many Chinese language models continue to run on U.S.‑designed processors. The evidence shows that chips from companies like Nvidia are increasingly deployed in data centers across Latin America, the Middle East, and Southeast Asia. These facilities are often operated by U.S. cloud providers such as Amazon, Microsoft, and Google, which together control more than half of the global cloud market.

## Chinese models find U.S. platforms abroad
When people hear that Chinese models are gaining users, they often assume those models run on Chinese chips. In reality, the latest DeepSeek model is being offered by at least 16 inference providers headquartered outside China, including Amazon Bedrock. This demonstrates that Chinese open‑weight large‑language models can be hosted on U.S. infrastructure, even though the models themselves are developed elsewhere.

## Open‑weight models versus proprietary LLMs
On the other side of the spectrum are proprietary LLMs such as Anthropic’s Claude or OpenAI’s GPT. Open‑weight models, like those from DeepSeek, can be customized for specialized tasks and run locally on user‑owned hardware. The distinction matters because U.S. policy aims to secure dominance in both the chip supply chain and the models that run on those chips.

## What this means for U.S. AI policy
AI preeminence will require global consumers and enterprises to choose U.S.‑developed LLMs over Chinese ones. The fact that Chinese models are already running on U.S. hardware challenges that goal. The Commerce Department’s new rule is a step toward tightening controls, but the underlying infrastructure remains widely accessible.

## Readers should watch
The next weeks will reveal whether the rule effectively limits foreign access to U.S. chips for AI inference. Analysts will also monitor how cloud providers adjust their offerings and whether Chinese model developers shift toward alternative hardware. The outcome will shape whether U.S. tech policy can maintain its intended advantage in the AI race.
