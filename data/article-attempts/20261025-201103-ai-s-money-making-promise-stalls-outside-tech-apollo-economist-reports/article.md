## AI Spending in Non‑Tech Firms Stays Flat
Apollo Global Management’s chief economist, Torsten Slok, released a note that casts doubt on the financial upside of artificial intelligence for most U.S. companies. He examined the S&P 500, excluding the seven dominant technology names—Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia, and Tesla—calling the remainder the S&P 493. For that group, Slok found that profit margins and revenue per worker have been essentially unchanged in the era of large‑scale AI investment. The data show no evidence that AI has boosted productivity or profitability for these non‑tech firms.

## The Magnificent 7 Keep Growing
In contrast, the seven excluded technology firms have seen both revenue per worker and profit margins rise since the AI spending wave began. Slok’s note highlights that the growth seen by the broader market is confined to these “Magnificent 7.” The top 10% of firms account for 99.5% of model‑serving and inference spend, and 80.4% of the highest‑spending companies are venture‑backed. The note points out that AI spending is highly concentrated among a small cluster of companies that depend on fresh capital rather than operating profits.

## Why the Payoff Is Still Zero for Most
Slok’s research suggests that, outside the tech sector, AI has delivered “zero payoff” to corporate America. The note stresses that despite massive investments and widespread hype, the technology has not translated into higher margins or revenue growth for the majority of companies. Apollo’s analysis, which drew on Bloomberg, Macrobond, and internal data, indicates that the productivity gains seen by the tech giants are not yet reflected in the broader corporate landscape.

## Implications for Corporate AI Budgets
The findings imply that many large enterprises may reconsider or scale back their AI spend. Apollo predicts that companies that have poured heavily into AI tools may soon pull back, as the financial benefits remain elusive. The research warns that the promise of an “age of abundance”—where AI would reduce the need for human labor—may be overstated for most businesses.

## Limitations and Uncertainty
The analysis relies on plotted lines and approximations; Apollo did not publish the underlying values. Therefore, while the overall trend is clear—flat margins for the S&P 493—the precise magnitude of any marginal improvements remains uncertain. Additionally, the concentration of AI spend among venture‑backed firms suggests that the broader economy’s exposure to AI risk may be limited to a small, high‑growth sector.

## What to Watch Next
Investors and corporate leaders should monitor whether the flat margins for the S&P 493 hold as AI adoption matures. If the broader market begins to show productivity gains, the narrative may shift. Until then, the evidence points to a scenario where AI’s financial impact is largely confined to the tech titans, leaving the rest of corporate America with no measurable payoff.
