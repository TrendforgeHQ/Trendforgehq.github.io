## Proposed $3 Billion Deal to Resolve School District Claims

YouTube is preparing to pay approximately $3 billion to resolve lawsuits from thousands of U.S. school districts, according to reporting from Bloomberg that cites people familiar with the matter. The legal actions center on allegations that the Google-owned video platform used an addictive design that negatively impacted the mental health of teenage students.

Sources indicate that YouTube reached the agreement in principle. However, the deal remains in a preliminary stage and is contingent on a majority of the participating school districts agreeing to the terms. Finalizing the deal officially will likely take several months. If accepted, the agreement will also require approval from Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers, who is presiding over the federal litigation.

## Platform Features and Impact on Schools

The school districts filing suit accuse YouTube, along with platforms like Meta, Snap, and TikTok, of designing features that encourage prolonged usage. According to claims cited by the American Psychological Association, the lawsuits focus specifically on platform design mechanics such as autoplay and infinite scrolling rather than the specific content users post. The districts allege that these design choices were made despite knowledge of the potential risks to younger audiences.

School systems report that these engagement features forced them to spend additional resources to address the consequences. Specifically, districts say they have been compelled to expand funding for student counseling and mental health services, as well as enforce stricter phone restrictions on campus. The pending federal lawsuit brings together claims from multiple school districts to address shared issues before trial, while still allowing each district's individual case to be decided separately.

## Broader Legal Context and Product Safeguards

Beyond financial terms, the reported settlement includes commitments to implement additional product safeguards for teenage users on YouTube. Reaching an agreement would allow Google to settle claims from participating districts and avoid drawn-out legal battles, particularly at a time when its products are widely used in classroom settings.

This tentative deal occurs alongside broader litigation involving major tech platforms. In a separate personal-injury legal battle over social media addiction, both YouTube and Meta were found negligent in March. YouTube also agreed to settle a separate personal-injury lawsuit in June, and previously settled a lawsuit in May alongside Meta, Snap, and TikTok involving a Kentucky school district, where YouTube agreed to pay slightly over $2 million. Under a related agreement, Meta's remaining $5.3 billion payout of a $17.1 billion settlement is contingent on YouTube and TikTok implementing matching payout amounts and similar product modifications.

## What the Evidence Shows

YouTube has reached an agreement in principle to pay about $3 billion to thousands of school districts to settle allegations of addictive product design. The deal requires approval from a majority of districts and the court, and it includes commitments for added teen safeguards. While this arrangement resolves claims from participating school districts, thousands of individual personal injury lawsuits against YouTube remain active.
