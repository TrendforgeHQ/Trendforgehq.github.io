## United’s Policy Shift
United Airlines announced it will no longer allow customers to use AI agents to book or manage flight reservations. The airline’s press release stated that sharing account credentials with third‑party AI assistants can expose sensitive information and lead to unintended purchases or mileage usage. United also warned that automated actions could violate Federal Aviation Administration and Department of Transportation rules, particularly the requirement for human acknowledgment that bags do not contain prohibited items.

## Contractual and Operational Details
In a recent contract update, United explicitly barred AI agents from creating, changing, or servicing tickets, and from buying seats, upgrades, or other products. While AI tools may still search and display fares, the traveler must personally complete the booking. United noted that it would treat any booking made through an AI interface as an Expedia reservation and would retain the right to cancel reservations without notice. Each AI query also incurs a cost for the airline, a factor United cited as part of its decision.

## Customer Acceptance and Industry Context
A fraud‑detection survey referenced by United found that 43 % of Americans would permit AI booking with final human approval, yet only 12 % would allow fully autonomous booking. United’s stance places it among a growing number of U.S. corporations restricting AI access to customer systems, reflecting heightened concerns about cybersecurity, privacy, and regulatory compliance. The airline’s action underscores the tension between innovative digital assistants and the need to safeguard sensitive travel data and uphold safety regulations.

## Implications for Travelers and Developers
For travelers, the policy means that any interaction with an AI‑powered travel agent must still conclude with a human‑initiated purchase. Developers of AI travel assistants must adjust their systems to prevent automated ticket transactions or to seek explicit user authorization before proceeding. Airlines and regulatory bodies may increasingly scrutinize AI tools that interact with aviation booking systems, potentially shaping future standards for digital identity verification and data sharing.

## Uncertainties and Next Steps
United has not outlined a timeline for reviewing this restriction, nor has it indicated whether future technological safeguards could allow limited AI involvement. The airline’s decision highlights the ongoing uncertainty surrounding AI integration in regulated sectors. Stakeholders should monitor United’s policy updates and any regulatory guidance that may emerge to clarify acceptable AI practices in travel booking.
