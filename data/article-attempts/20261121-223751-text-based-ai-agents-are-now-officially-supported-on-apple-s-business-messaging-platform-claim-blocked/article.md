## A New Era for Business Messaging
In June 2026, Apple granted approval to Poke, making it the first AI agent to run directly inside Apple Messages for Business. The service can be accessed both through a dedicated app and via ordinary text messages. Users can send text, voice notes, photos, or forwarded emails, and the assistant can act on their behalf by making calls, sending emails and managing follow‑ups.

## How Text‑Based Agents Work
Rather than downloading another app, a growing number of agents can simply be texted like an ordinary person. When a user sends a prompt, the agent processes it and returns an answer or initiates an action.The service is designed to work across both an app and text messages, allowing users to send text messages, voice notes, photos, or forwarded emails and have the assistant take actions such as making calls, sending emails, and managing follow‑ups.

## The Live‑Chat Preview Problem
Live chat agents on websites can see everything, including messages that have not yet been sent, because of the live typing preview feature. A feature known as live‑typing preview or real‑time typing view allows customer‑service agents to see what users are typing before they hit “Send.” The LiveAgent system, used by more than 40 000 companies, states that this preview lets agents anticipate questions and deliver faster responses. Because agents can read messages before they are sent, users should avoid typing personal information into chat boxes, even on secure devices.

## Industry Context
Google announced an “agentic era” for its Gemini line in spring 2025, and other major players such as Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic have also released their own agents. Apple’s approval of Poke signals that the trend toward integrated AI assistants is moving beyond research and into mainstream commercial use.

## Take‑away
Apple’s endorsement of Poke demonstrates that AI agents can now operate seamlessly within standard text‑messaging environments, expanding the range of tasks they can perform. At the same time, the prevalence of live‑typing previews on web chat widgets highlights a privacy concern that users should be aware of.
