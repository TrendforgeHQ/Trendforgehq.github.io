## The AI Debate Surfaces in a Data‑Center‑Focused Race
Wisconsin’s 2026 gubernatorial race has long focused on the construction of data centers.  Yet on October 14, Democratic nominee David Crowley shifted the conversation to the regulation of artificial intelligence.  Crowley unveiled an 11‑point plan that would impose guardrails on AI products for minors and hold technology companies accountable for scams and fraudulent activity on their platforms.

## Crowley’s Regulatory Blueprint
Crowley’s proposal, shared with Wisconsin Watch, calls for safeguards that target minors and protect vulnerable populations.  The plan would require state legislative action to enforce these guardrails, according to the announcement.  In a statement, Crowley cited personal concerns: he and his wife are raising three daughters in the age of social media and AI, and worries about strangers messaging children, fake images circulating in schools, or scams draining grandparents’ savings.  He said, “Like so many parents, Ericka and I are raising our three girls in the age of social media and AI, and we’re navigating things no generation before us has ever had to, worrying about whether a stranger is messaging our kids, or a fake picture of our daughter is going around school, or even scammers trying to drain a grandparent’s life savings.”

## Parallel Calls for Protection from the GOP
Republican candidate Tom Tiffany echoed Crowley’s emphasis on protecting children and older adults.  Tiffany stated that Wisconsin needs “commonsense guardrails” to safeguard minors and seniors, holding companies accountable for platforms that allow the creation of sexually explicit images or engage minors in inappropriate chatbot conversations.  Tiffany also noted that he and the rest of Wisconsin’s congressional delegation voted for the 2025 bill that addresses social media addiction.

## Context: A Recent Poll and Campaign Dynamics
A Badger Battleground Poll conducted in August gave Crowley a narrow lead over Tiffany, 48% to 44%.  The poll also showed 43% of respondents viewing Tiffany unfavorably, compared with 26% viewing Crowley unfavorably.  Crowley’s campaign emphasized that Democrats should unite to defeat Tiffany in the general election.  At Crowley’s watch party in Milwaukee, some attendees expressed doubt that he could attract voters who previously supported former Republican candidate Hong.

## Legal and Financial Implications
In August, Attorney General Josh Kaul announced that Wisconsin could receive up to $313 million from a settlement with Meta, a social media company.  The settlement followed allegations that Meta’s addictive platform features harmed teens’ mental health.  The proposed AI regulations would dovetail with this broader legal scrutiny of digital platforms.

## What’s Next for Wisconsin?
The state legislature would need to act to implement Crowley’s AI guardrails.  Meanwhile, the campaign remains focused on data‑center construction, but the AI debate could influence voter priorities, especially among families concerned about digital safety.  Observers should watch how the legislature responds to both Crowley’s proposal and the federal settlement with Meta as the election cycle advances.
