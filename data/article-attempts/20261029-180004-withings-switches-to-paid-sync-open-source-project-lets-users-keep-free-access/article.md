## What Changed at Withings
Withings announced that accounts created on or after October 13, 2026 must subscribe to the paid Withings Plus tier to keep automatic data sync with third‑party apps. The move applies to both Apple Health and Android users who rely on Google Health Connect.

## User Reaction and Impact
Customers expressed frustration, noting that the change erodes confidence in the ecosystem and raises doubts about future support. One user, who owns a Withings Body Smart scale, said they would hesitate to buy new Withings products because of the paywall, despite grandfathering existing accounts. Sentiment in the support thread echoed this view.

## A Workaround: YourOwnWeigh
In response to the announcement, Tom Anthony—already dissatisfied with Withings’ mobile app—created an open‑source project named YourOwnWeigh. The web app captures Bluetooth data from older Withings scale models, patches the device firmware, and routes the information to a user‑chosen server instead of Withings’ cloud. Users can then sync the data to any compatible third‑party app for free. The project currently supports only two older scale models.

## What Users Can Do Now
Existing Withings accounts remain grandfathered and can continue to sync automatically without a subscription. All users can still export their historical records as CSV or PDF at no cost. For those who wish to avoid the new paywall, the YourOwnWeigh solution offers a free alternative, provided they have one of the supported scale models.

## Looking Ahead
Withings’ policy shift may prompt more users to seek third‑party solutions or to consider competitors that keep free syncing. The open‑source community’s response demonstrates that users can reclaim control over their health data even when a company changes its business model. The long‑term effect on Withings’ ecosystem will depend on how many users adopt such workarounds and whether the company expands support beyond the two older scale models.
