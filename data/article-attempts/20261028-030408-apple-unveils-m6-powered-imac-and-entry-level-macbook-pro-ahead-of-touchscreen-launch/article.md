## New M6‑Powered Mac Lineup
Apple announced a 24‑inch iMac and a 14‑inch entry‑level MacBook Pro, both powered by the new M6 silicon. The iMac is described as five times faster than the 2024 model in artificial‑intelligence tasks, while the MacBook Pro is 30 % faster for graphics, according to Apple’s own performance claims. The new machines are part of an end‑to‑end overhaul of the company’s computer line.

## Target Audiences and Pricing
Apple’s marketing signals that the MacBook Pro line is aimed at users ranging from college students to business professionals and creators, whereas the iMac is positioned for families and small‑business customers. The iMac starts at RMB 12,499 and the MacBook Pro at RMB 15,999. The pricing reflects the entry‑level nature of the M6‑based devices.

## Context Within Apple’s Product Rhythm
The launch of the M6 Macs follows a flurry of announcements this fall, including a new iPad mini and a series of hardware refreshes. Apple has increased its launch cadence in the second half of the year, unveiling new processors, desktops, AirPods, watches, and more. The company’s Mac segment accounted for $33.7 billion of its $416.1 billion 2025 revenue, making it the fourth‑largest sales segment.

## Upcoming Touchscreen MacBook Pro
Apple is expected to conclude the week with the debut of its first‑ever touchscreen‑equipped MacBook Pro. The new device will feature an OLED display, offering deeper blacks and higher contrast than the mini‑LED panels in current models. The OLED panel could raise starting prices by up to $500 compared with the $2,499 baseline for the 14‑inch model in the US.

## Future Roadmap Highlights
Beyond the touchscreen push, Apple plans refreshed MacBook Neo and MacBook Air models for the first half of 2027. In 2028, the company intends to introduce an M7 Ultra chip and a new Mac Studio. Meanwhile, it continues to sell higher‑end M5 Pro and M5 Max MacBook Pros, which contain more advanced processing and graphics cores than the entry‑level M6 machines.

## Practical Takeaways
For consumers, the M6 iMac offers a significant AI performance boost, making it attractive for tasks such as machine‑learning inference or AI‑driven content creation. The 30 % graphics speed increase in the entry‑level MacBook Pro may appeal to students and pros who need decent GPU performance without the premium price of the higher‑end models.

The forthcoming touchscreen MacBook Pro signals a shift toward hybrid laptop‑desktop experiences. Users who rely on touch input or wish to use a MacBook as a tablet‑style device will likely watch for that launch. However, the higher cost of OLED panels and the limited availability of the touchscreen models may constrain early adoption.

## What’s Still Uncertain
Apple has not yet disclosed detailed specifications for the upcoming touchscreen MacBook Pro, such as battery life, weight, or exact pricing. The company has also not clarified how the new OLED panels will integrate with the existing 14‑inch and 16‑inch lineups, beyond the possibility of price increases. Finally, while the M6 chip delivers impressive AI and graphics gains, performance in real‑world applications remains to be seen.

## Bottom Line
Apple’s latest M6‑powered iMac and entry‑level MacBook Pro represent a clear push into AI‑centric desktop performance while maintaining a broad appeal across consumer segments. The company’s planned touchscreen MacBook Pro, with its OLED display and anticipated price hike, may redefine the MacBook experience for users who value touch input. As Apple expands its lineup through 2027 and 2028, observers should track how these new devices perform in everyday workloads and how the company balances price and capability across its desktop ecosystem.
