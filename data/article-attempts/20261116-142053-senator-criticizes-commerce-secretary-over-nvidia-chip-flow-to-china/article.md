## The Letter That Sparks the Debate
On a recent letter to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, Senator Maria Cantwell, a leading Democrat on the Senate commerce committee, charged the Department of Commerce (DOC) with neglecting to enforce protections meant to keep Nvidia’s high‑end AI chips out of China. Cantwell’s letter also criticizes the DOC for allowing Alibaba and ByteDance to acquire Nvidia chips through overseas data centers and for extending a loophole that enabled restricted companies to obtain U.S. technology through subsidiaries. The letter was sent just before a high‑profile meeting between President Trump and President Xi Jinping.

## Alleged Loopholes and Business Ties
Cantwell points to a conflict of interest: the financial services firm Cantor Fitzgerald, formerly led by Lutnick and now steered by his sons, reportedly held $1.2 billion in Nvidia stock at the time of Lutnick’s nomination for Commerce Secretary. The DOC has been accused of “putting business interests ahead of national security.”

## The Commerce Department’s Response
In response to questions about these allegations, Commerce spokeswoman Kristen Eichamer told CNBC that Lutnick had “no such conversation” in the Situation Room and that Lutnick had proactively agreed to appear voluntarily before the Banking Committee to address questions. The DOC did not respond to CNBC’s request for comment on Bessent’s remarks regarding chip leakage.

## Treasury’s Take on Leakage
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, in a separate interview, acknowledged that “there is a lot of leakage in the system” and that the Treasury is working to close it down. He said the Megaspeed blacklisting has stalled because Commerce “has the pen.” Bessent also noted that the story has so far focused on Jensen Huang’s personal foibles.

## Congressional Momentum
Both parties are moving toward tighter AI chip export rules. House Foreign Affairs Chairman Brian Mast has asked Jeff Kessler, Commerce’s under‑secretary for industry and security, to appear before his committee on December 1. Meanwhile, a new initiative is expected to designate Nvidia, OpenAI, Anthropic, Amazon, and Microsoft as AI “national champions,” granting them expedited export license approvals to help them compete with Chinese technology firms.

## What This Means for U.S. Export Controls
The allegations suggest that existing export control mechanisms may be insufficient to prevent advanced AI chips from reaching Chinese firms that are prohibited or restricted. The DOC’s handling of the Megaspeed blacklisting, the alleged loophole for subsidiaries, and the apparent lack of enforcement could allow U.S. technology to bolster China’s AI development.

## Next Steps to Watch
Readers should monitor the outcomes of the upcoming committee hearings, the DOC’s actions regarding the Megaspeed blacklist, and any new legislation that could strengthen export controls. The timing of the presidential meeting with Xi Jinping may also influence policy discussions. Observing how the DOC balances business interests with national security concerns will be crucial for understanding future trade and technology policy.
