## A New Budget Player
Apple’s announcement of the MacBook Neo marks the company’s first foray into the low‑end PC market. Priced at $599, the Neo is powered by an A18 processor, the same chip found in the iPhone 16 Pro. This configuration positions the laptop against budget PCs and Chromebooks—segments in which Apple has historically had minimal presence.

The Neo’s entry‑level status also allows Apple to push price increases on its higher‑tier laptops. The recently announced MacBook Air M5, MacBook Pro, and Pro Max M5 saw price hikes of $100 to $400, moving those models further into premium territory to make room for the affordable Neo.

Continuity features with the iPhone may persuade existing PC users to switch to the Neo. By integrating seamlessly with the broader Apple ecosystem, the device offers a compelling reason for users already invested in iOS to consider a macOS experience.

## The Long‑Anticipated Touchscreen Pro
Rumors of a touchscreen MacBook Pro have circulated for years, but recent reports have tightened the timeline. An event slated for October 28, 2026, is expected to feature hands‑on demonstrations rather than a formal keynote. TechRadar notes that a significant update to the MacBook Pro family is likely next week, delivering a touchscreen laptop.

The proposed models include new 14‑inch and 16‑inch versions. Both are anticipated to pair the first touchscreen on a Mac with an OLED display, offering a vibrant, rich viewing experience similar to that of an OLED iPhone or iPad Pro. The design is described as slimmer and redesigned compared to current models.

Engadget’s report confirms that Apple has a concrete time frame for the touchscreen debut, a milestone that has been discussed since 2023. This update would represent the first time a MacBook offers a touch interface, following a decade of deliberate avoidance.

## Design and Ergonomics
Steve Jobs famously dismissed touchscreen laptops as “ergonomically terrible,” a sentiment that has guided Apple’s design philosophy for more than a decade. The new 14‑inch MacBook Pro is expected to incorporate a PROLED XDR display while weighing just 3 pounds—lighter than the current generation of non‑touch MacBook Pros, which start at 3.4 pounds for the newly introduced M6 models.

Apple has reportedly invested effort into making macOS more touch‑friendly. Early observations suggest that touch works almost exactly as one would expect with Apple’s first‑party apps, indicating a smoother user experience.

The company’s focus on lightness and accessibility aligns with its historical brand identity. While the new models introduce color options for the iMac, the laptops have traditionally remained monochrome.

## What to Watch
The MacBook Neo’s competitive pricing could pressure Windows and Chromebook manufacturers to reconsider their low‑budget offerings. Meanwhile, the upcoming touchscreen Pro will test Apple’s ability to integrate a new form factor into its long‑standing design language.

Key questions remain: Will the Neo’s performance match the price point? How will the Pro’s touch interface affect workflow for professionals accustomed to a trackpad? And can Apple maintain its lightweight ethos while adding a new input method?

Apple’s forthcoming events and product releases will provide the answers. For now, the market is poised for a shift toward more affordable, ecosystem‑centric laptops and a potential redesign of the professional MacBook line.
