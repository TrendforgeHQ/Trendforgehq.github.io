OpenAI’s latest public move—stopping internal trials of the Astra model and expanding a cybersecurity‑focused program—signals that the company is grappling with the dual promise and risk of advanced AI.

## Astra’s Development and Halt
OpenAI announced that it has temporarily halted some internal activities involving Astra because of fears that the model could pose a cyber threat. The company said its early evaluations of Astra show performance strong enough that it cannot rule out a Critical capability level at this time, and that it has implemented universal monitoring for risky actions and misalignment across all agentic applications of Astra, including training and evaluation.

## Broader Security Context
The pause comes amid a wave of security incidents involving major AI labs. U.S. lawmakers are pushing an “AI Kill Switch” bill that would require AI companies to keep the ability to shut down, throttle or suspend their models. Representative Ted Lieu highlighted the need to move the bill through this year because advanced closed‑weight models are already enabling unauthorized hacks of other companies.

## OpenAI’s Cybersecurity Initiative
OpenAI’s cybersecurity effort is part of a broader strategy. The company has launched an application security AI agent called Codex Security, a cybersecurity grants program that began in 2023, and has made a donation to the Linux Foundation to support open‑source security. It also introduced a “Preparedness Framework” designed to assess and defend against severe harm from frontier AI capabilities.

## Daybreak Red and Real‑World Testing
OpenAI has opened its Daybreak program to a select group of thousands of cybersecurity professionals. The program provides access to ChatGPT Cyber, a model designed to help with vulnerability hunting and development. In a recent test, the model helped identify 948 potential issues in the Linux kernel and 81 novel vulnerabilities in the Firefox web browser.

## Industry Response
Competitor Anthropic recently announced that its Claude Mythos Preview model is only being privately released for now because it could be exploited by hackers and bad actors. Anthropic also launched an industry coalition that includes competitors such as Google to discuss how generative‑AI advances will affect cybersecurity. These developments have prompted debate among security experts about whether the industry is allocating enough resources to address emerging threats.

## What Readers Should Watch
The current state of AI security hinges on how quickly companies can balance rapid model development with rigorous testing and controls. OpenAI’s pause on Astra and its expanded Daybreak program illustrate a growing trend toward embedding security safeguards early in the model lifecycle. Watch for updates on the “AI Kill Switch” bill and how other labs respond to the Anthropic and OpenAI announcements.

## Limitations and Uncertainty
While OpenAI’s new cybersecurity model has identified thousands of potential vulnerabilities, it is unclear whether frontier labs are devoting enough resources to fully address the broader spectrum of threats. The effectiveness of the “AI Kill Switch” bill and the industry coalition’s impact remain to be seen as the debate continues.
