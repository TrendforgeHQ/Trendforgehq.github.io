## Connected Apps Platform Enters Preview Stage

Google has introduced the Connected Apps Platform preview, giving businesses a unified path to bring software capabilities directly into Gemini and Google Search. The company's goal is to allow any business or developer to reach users through these AI interfaces by submitting connected applications and monitoring their ongoing performance. Under this approach, a single build covers both Gemini and Search, allowing developers to choose whether an application appears in Search, the Gemini app, or across both environments.

Several services are already operating or scheduled to launch within these surfaces. Search currently features fewer integrations than Gemini, offering access to tools such as Canva, YouTube Music, Instacart, and Yahoo Fantasy Football. For example, users can connect to Canva to design a flyer or interact with YouTube Music to build a playlist. Google also identified the meditation app Calm as another new partner in this effort, noting that Calm will soon launch on both Search and Gemini. Additions to Google Search are set to roll out over the next few weeks.

## Developer Integration Mechanics and System Architecture

To build for the platform, developers begin by integrating with either a cloud-hosted Model Context Protocol (MCP) server or an Android MCP server using AppFunctions APIs. After technical integration, developers use the Google Connected Console to register, test, and submit their software. Google accepts the standard MCP format rather than requiring a proprietary structure. In principle, this technical framework enables a company to reuse a server it already operates for other AI assistants. 

Every submission undergoes a formal review process before going live. Once approval is granted by Google, the developer—rather than Google—decides the precise moment the application is published. Access to the platform is opening in stages, and Google has not shared when the framework will become more broadly available.

## Platform Uncertainties and Partner App Discrepancies

While Google has outlined the developer workflow, several commercial and operational details remain unannounced. The company has not provided details regarding platform fees, user data handling terms, or market availability. It is also unclear how Google will determine app ranking or select which specific connected application handles a user's request when competing options exist.

There are also conflicting accounting metrics regarding the total number of connected applications across Google's surfaces. While Google announced that the Gemini app connects to more than 40 apps on October 13, earlier documentation presents different tallies. On August 12, PPC Land reported that Google's availability help article already listed 41 connected apps prior to an announcement of 13 named partners, five of which were already included on the initial list. 

Google's recent post counts Search and Gemini integrations together without clarifying whether an application available on both surfaces is counted once or twice. Furthermore, the arithmetic derived from Google's earlier public lists indicates a total for the Gemini app alone that already exceeds 50. Google's post leaves the broader application count harder to place accurately.

## What the Evidence Shows

Google has launched a preview of its Connected Apps Platform using standard Model Context Protocol servers to bring third-party app functionality into Gemini and Search. Developers retain control over launch timing after passing review, though Search currently maintains fewer integrations than Gemini. Significant details remain unannounced, including developer fees, ranking criteria, data policy terms, and broad availability schedules.
