## Binance Taps Corporate Treasury to Replace Stolen Crypto

Following a record-breaking security breach that resulted in the theft of $6.3 billion from Binance users, the cryptocurrency exchange announced it has replenished approximately 90 percent of the missing digital assets. According to statements from the company's CEO and official updates, roughly $5.7 billion in assets has been replaced as part of an effort to cover the remaining balance entirely so that customers do not bear the financial impact of the event.

To replace the missing tokens, Binance tapped its corporate treasury to purchase billions of dollars in cryptocurrency directly from open markets and private brokers. The company also drew from its existing emergency fund—which held around $1 billion prior to the event but was rendered insufficient due to the unprecedented scale of the heist—alongside its insurance funds. This effort represents progress from an earlier update where the exchange reported covering roughly 80 percent of the lost assets.

## Sourcing Liquid Assets and Tapping Emergency Insurance

Rebuilding the compromised account balances required Binance to acquire a variety of digital assets. In a social media post, the platform confirmed it has been buying back affected tokens, including Bitcoin, Zcash, and the stablecoins USDT and USDC. Binance stated it will continue acquiring tokens through market channels and over-the-counter (OTC) trades until 100 percent of all relevant assets are restored.

However, securing the final 10 percent of the funds presents ongoing challenges. Binance noted that the remaining unreplaced tokens are less liquid, making them significantly harder to source and purchase in bulk. To help absorb the massive financial hit, the exchange utilized its Futures Insurance Fund, a reserve designed by regulatory requirements to protect traders from severe losses during extreme market volatility.

## Attack Context and Ongoing Investigation

The security incident began on a Thursday around 4 p.m., when Binance detected a breach on its blockchain network that it initially attributed to a compromised employee laptop. Following the breach, two days passed before analysts suggested that North Korean hackers were likely responsible for the attack. The same group was previously named by the FBI in 2025 for a $1.5 billion hack against Bybit, another cryptocurrency exchange.

Despite analyst conclusions, Binance has not publicly confirmed who carried out the intrusion and stated that its internal investigation remains active. Meanwhile, the market reacted sharply to the news. The broader industry experienced shockwaves, and Bitcoin fell below $77,000, though it later cut some of its initial losses.

## Timeline for User Withdrawals and Proof of Reserves

Restoring normal exchange operations is currently underway as Binance takes steps to rebuild market confidence. The company stated it is working to restore withdrawal services in phases, aiming to have withdrawals operational by Sunday. 

Additionally, Binance announced plans to publish an updated Proof of Reserves report by Monday, November 2. This report is intended to allow users to directly verify the digital assets supporting their specific account balances on the platform.

## What the Evidence Shows

Binance has replaced 90 percent of the $6.3 billion stolen in a major security breach, using corporate treasury funds, emergency reserves, and its Futures Insurance Fund to shield users from financial loss. The exchange is actively acquiring the remaining 10 percent of less liquid tokens via market and OTC channels while working to restore withdrawal services in phases by Sunday. While analysts linked the breach to North Korean hackers, Binance's investigation remains ongoing, with an updated Proof of Reserves report planned for Monday, November 2.
