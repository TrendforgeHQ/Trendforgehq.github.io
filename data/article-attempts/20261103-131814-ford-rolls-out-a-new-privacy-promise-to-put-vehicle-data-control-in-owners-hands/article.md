## Ford’s Explicit Privacy Commitment
Ford Motor Co. has launched a new customer‑facing program that it calls the Privacy Promise.  The program publicly commits to keeping vehicle data safe and to giving owners control over the information they share.

The company says the Promise will be presented on a dedicated website that details its privacy practices and the controls available to users.  The site will also explain how Ford designs, builds, and manages data from collection through storage and disposal.

Ford’s chief privacy officer, Kristin Jones, explained that the company’s goal is to offer products and services that provide value to customers without monetizing personal information.  She said the company never sells driving data to third‑party data brokers.

## How the Promise Works
Under the Promise, Ford supplies vehicle privacy settings that let owners decide which data they wish to share.  These settings apply to the features and services that customers choose to use.

Ford says it uses privacy‑enhancing technologies, such as anonymizing or aggregating data, to protect user privacy further.  The privacy settings are intended to be simple to access and adjust so that users can change their preferences at any time.

The program is part of Ford’s broader approach to privacy, which the company says involves building privacy into its products from the beginning.  It has conducted a survey of more than 1,000 U.S. consumers, both Ford and non‑Ford owners, and found that while privacy is important, customers also see value in data sharing.

## Timing and Context
Ford announced the Promise within days of General Motors agreeing to a $220 million settlement over how it handled driver information.  The settlement was related to GM’s Smart Driver program, which enrolled millions of motorists from 2016 until 2024.

An outside party will audit Ford’s privacy programs to provide independent oversight, according to the reporting.  It is not yet clear whether owners who were impacted by GM’s settlement will receive any portion of the $220 million.

## Market Roll‑Out
Ford plans to roll out privacy resources first for Lincoln customers and for users in Mexico and Canada within the next few weeks.  Additional markets are expected to follow over time.  The launch reflects the growing importance of transparency as connected vehicle technology becomes more widespread.

## What This Means for Drivers
Ford’s new program is designed to give drivers more agency over the data that their vehicles generate.  By offering clear settings, a dedicated privacy website, and a public commitment to not sell driving data, Ford is addressing growing consumer concerns about data security and privacy.

The program also aligns with recent regulatory and industry scrutiny, as automakers face pressure to protect personal information while still leveraging data for services.  Ford’s approach may influence how other manufacturers structure their own privacy offerings.

## Next Steps
Owners who own Ford or Lincoln vehicles can expect to find the Privacy Promise website and new settings soon.  The company will likely expand the program to additional markets and may introduce further privacy tools as it receives feedback from users and auditors.

Consumers should keep an eye on how Ford implements the promise and whether the promised controls are truly easy to use.  Transparency reports and audit outcomes will also help determine whether the company meets its stated commitments.

Ford’s Privacy Promise shows a tangible shift toward customer‑centric privacy practices in the automotive sector, a move that may set a new standard for how vehicle data is managed and shared.
