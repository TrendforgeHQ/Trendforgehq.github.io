## JPMorgan’s Ten‑Year Outlook
JPMorgan Chase’s asset‑management division released a projection that Bitcoin will yield a 10% annual return over the next decade, surpassing U.S. large‑cap stocks (7.5%), broad U.S. bonds (5.6%), and gold (5.5%). The model bases this expectation on Bitcoin’s supply curve, portfolio role, and past performance.

## Key Drivers and Caveats
The firm highlights Bitcoin’s inclusion in diversified portfolios as the second‑most important factor, carrying a 30% weight. It also cites a 10‑year compound annual growth rate of 63% for Bitcoin and notes that the next mining reward halving is expected around April 2028, reinforcing scarcity arguments. While the bank acknowledges that volatility has declined, it describes Bitcoin as a speculative asset and a hedge against monetary easing, and it warns that unstable correlations may limit its effectiveness as a diversifier.

## Market Context
The report is mirrored in coverage from The Motley Fool, which confirms the same return expectations and acknowledges the bank’s cautious stance. Both sources note that the figures are unlikely to materialize exactly by 2036, but the underlying drivers—supply constraints, portfolio weight, and historical growth—are difficult to dispute. The analysis also notes that the selected U.S. stocks could deliver significant returns, suggesting that Bitcoin’s projected performance is not trivial.

## What Investors Should Watch
Investors interested in long‑term asset allocation may view Bitcoin’s projected 10% return as noteworthy relative to traditional assets, but they should remain mindful of the bank’s characterization of the asset as speculative and the potential for correlation volatility. Monitoring Bitcoin’s supply events, such as the upcoming halving, and the evolving role of the cryptocurrency in institutional portfolios can provide signals about whether the forecast gains traction.

## Bottom Line
JPMorgan Chase’s projection places Bitcoin ahead of stocks, bonds, and gold for the next ten years, driven by supply dynamics and portfolio inclusion. The forecast is tempered by the bank’s own caution about volatility and speculation, underscoring that while the numbers are compelling, they remain predictions subject to market uncertainty.
