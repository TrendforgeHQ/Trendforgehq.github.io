## Exit From Bitcoin Mining
Core Scientific’s November 5 earnings call revealed CEO Adam Sullivan said the company expects to finish its exit from Bitcoin mining by year‑end. CFO Jim Nygaard added that in 2027 the firm will operate as a pure‑play colocation provider, renting data‑center space and power exclusively to AI customers.The announcement announced that Core Scientific plans to complete its exit from Bitcoin mining by year‑end.

## Revenue Shift and Colocation Growth
Colocation now accounts for about 92% of Core Scientific’s revenue, up from 83.2% in the previous quarter and a large jump from 18.4% a year earlier.9 million included $215.4 million from colocation services. The company’s billing capacity exceeds $700 million in annualized colocation revenue. In contrast, self‑mining revenue fell sharply to $12.3 million from $57.4 million, showing a significant decline in Bitcoin‑related income.

## Financial Context and Risks
Core Scientific carries roughly $4.3 billion of long‑term debt, meaning any delivery delays would primarily impact shareholders. If the company misses its year‑end exit date, it will likely remain dependent on a single tenant longer, weakening its diversification strategy.

## Market Implications
The shift highlights how hyperscaler and GPU customers are driving a rotation into digital‑infrastructure stocks, with benefits accruing to firms that can serve AI workloads rather than traditional cryptocurrency mining. Core Scientific’s pivot may influence other crypto‑focused data‑center operators to reevaluate their revenue models.

## Takeaway
Core Scientific’s move to exit Bitcoin mining and focus on AI colocation underscores the volatility of mining revenue tied to Bitcoin prices and network difficulty. Investors should monitor the company’s debt load, tenant concentration, and the timing of its transition to gauge the success of this strategic shift.
