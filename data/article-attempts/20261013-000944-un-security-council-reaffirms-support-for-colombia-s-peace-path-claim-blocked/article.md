## Colombia’s Political Milestone
In a recent Security Council meeting, the Secretary‑General’s Special Representative for Colombia, Miroslav Jenča, praised the country’s peaceful electoral process. He noted that the March legislative elections proceeded without adverse security incidents, demonstrating strong national institutions.

## Reinforced Peace Architecture
Jenča announced that the Verification Mission has been fully restructured to reflect a refocused mandate and streamlined operations in line with UN80 reductions. The Mission remains ready to assist Colombia along its path to peace, emphasizing that it can contribute substantially through the Agreement’s instruments and its presence in conflict‑affected regions.

## Civil Society’s Voice
The Representative highlighted the Council’s renewed practice of including civil society voices in its Colombia discussions, expressing pleasure at this inclusion. He also thanked Her Excellency Rosa Villavicencio, Colombia’s Minister of Foreign Affairs, who exemplifies the significant role women have played in building peace.

## Ongoing Challenges and Moral Stance
While Colombia has become more peaceful than during the conflict’s peak years, the Representative condemned the increased recruitment and use of children by armed groups. He warned that whoever assumes the presidency will face formidable challenges consolidating peace and security in regions still affected by conflict.

## Digital Innovation and Future Engagement
The Mission is investing in digital innovation to optimise field analysis and verification. Jenča underscored the need for sustained international engagement to overcome remaining obstacles, deepen gains, and build a more peaceful and secure future for all Colombians.

## What the Evidence Shows
The Verification Mission stands ready to continue to assist the Colombians along this path.

These facts underline that while progress is evident, ongoing support and vigilance are essential to sustain peace and security in Colombia.
