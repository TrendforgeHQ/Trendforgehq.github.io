## The Intersection of Blockchain and Artificial Intelligence

Ethereum co-founder Joseph Lubin stated on CNBC that while artificial intelligence can offer creativity and expertise, it is unable to create trust on its own. In his view, blockchains like Ethereum can supply that missing trust layer. This dynamic sets the stage for AI and cryptocurrency to eventually merge, forming a unified system where programmatic work can be commissioned, executed, and financially compensated.

Under this potential framework, Ethereum's capital base is expected to gradually attract AI agents over time, with underlying payments primarily flowing through Layer-2 networks. While this full integration remains an evolving proposition, there are indications that Lubin's conceptual framework is already beginning to take shape across the decentralised ecosystem.

## Standards, Capital, and Agent Growth

One of Ethereum's primary contributions to the emerging AI-in-crypto sector is ERC-8004. This new blockchain standard is designed to track both the identities and reputations of AI agents operating across network environments. Establishing verifiable identities and reputation metrics provides an essential foundation for automated systems conducting autonomous transactions.

In addition to technical standards, network activity suggests expanding agent adoption. As of May 13 of this year, Ethereum hosted approximately 32,343 registered agents, reflecting a count that has more than doubled over a five-month span. Moving forward, the network's capital reserves are expected to draw in agents native to other ecosystems, while encouraging more developers to build and launch their AI agents directly on Ethereum.

Financial scale remains another core anchor for this network activity. Ethereum holds $49.6 billion in capital stored within its decentralized finance (DeFi) applications, alongside more than $144 billion in stablecoins. These deep liquidity pools represent resources that autonomous agents will likely need to utilize to settle transactions and execute automated operations.

## Transaction Dynamics and Long-Term Outlook

Despite the growing number of registered agents and available liquidity, current transaction data shows that agent-driven fee generation remains in an early stage. Over the 30 days ending in October, Ethereum collected $17.7 million in total transaction fees. However, automated agents supplied almost none of that sum, as Ethereum's main chain had settled just 644 agent payments as of October.

Payment volumes across alternative standards also highlight recent volatility. Monthly agent payments using x402—a standard for facilitating stablecoin transactions—peaked at $20.5 million in November 2025 before declining to $263,863 in September 2026. These fluctuating transaction figures demonstrate that practical adoption can experience significant swings as infrastructure evolves.

For investors and market participants, these network dynamics mean Ethereum offers a direct mechanism to add AI exposure to a crypto portfolio, even if agent-driven transaction fees take years to fully ramp up. ## What the Evidence Shows

Joseph Lubin views blockchain trust as a necessary bridge for AI systems to commission and compensate programmatic work. While Ethereum holds significant liquidity and a growing registry of over 32,000 agents, mainnet agent transaction volume remains minimal. Consequently, the network provides AI exposure, though meaningful fee generation from automated agents may take years to materialize.
