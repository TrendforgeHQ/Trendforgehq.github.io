## The departure that rattled Nvidia’s open‑source strategy
Nvidia’s CEO Jensen Huang stepped down after admitting to an affair with a person linked to alleged Chinese intelligence. The resignation came as the company had not yet closed a $13 billion deal to acquire the open‑source platform Hugging Face, and it had just agreed to pay $6 billion for a non‑exclusive license from the AI‑coding start‑up Poolside.
Huang had long framed open‑source AI as essential for safety, innovation, diffusion, and sovereignty, and his leadership had driven investments in startups such as Reflection AI and Thinking Machines Lab. His departure raises questions about the future of those initiatives and about whether the board will pursue the Hugging Face acquisition.

## Interim leadership and a potential shift in priorities
Nvidia named CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO. It is unclear how she will handle the Hugging Face negotiation or the broader support for the open‑model ecosystem. Historically, founder‑driven priorities tend to receive a downgrade when leadership changes, suggesting that the new CEO may not pursue the same level of commitment to open source that Huang championed.
Inside the company, an insider told TechCrunch that the leadership change is unlikely to affect the development of Nvidia’s Nemotron family of open‑weight AI models, which were positioned as competitors to closed‑source frontier models. However, the board’s stance on the Hugging Face deal remains uncertain.

## Employee and investor reactions
An internal suggestion box post, submitted before Huang’s resignation, urged the board to consider employee sentiment. Since then, more than 1,600 comments have appeared on the post, with several employees demanding transparency about the exit and expressing concerns that the company could suffer without Huang at the helm. A petition drawing over 176,000 signatures—including more than 92,000 shareholders and figures such as Elon Musk and Shopify CEO Tobias Lütke—has called for his return.
Despite the push, a source told CNBC that Nvidia’s board is not considering bringing Huang back as CEO. The board has engaged the law firm Wachtell Lipton to investigate whether Huang’s conduct compromised national security or company secrets.

## What the future might look like
If Kress maintains the current trajectory, Nvidia could continue to advance its open‑source initiatives through existing agreements, but the company may also slow or alter the Hugging Face acquisition. Investors have noted that Huang’s absence could affect Nvidia’s valuation, given his role in steering the company’s explosive growth and its expansion into the AI movement.
The impact on the open‑source ecosystem is mixed. While internal development of projects like Nemotron is expected to proceed, the broader strategic push—such as pushing back against Washington’s attempts to restrict open AI—may wane without Huang’s outspoken leadership.

## Key takeaways for readers
- Nvidia’s CEO transition follows Huang’s resignation amid national‑security concerns and a pending high‑profile acquisition. 
- The new interim CEO, Colette Kress, may not prioritize the same open‑source agenda that defined Huang’s tenure. 
- Employees and investors have actively petitioned for Huang’s return, but the board has ruled it out. 
- Project development such as Nemotron is likely to continue, but the company’s broader open‑source strategy could shift, affecting the Hugging Face deal and its stance on regulatory pushback. 

Readers should watch how Kress navigates the Hugging Face negotiations, the outcome of the legal probe, and any shifts in Nvidia’s investment in open‑source startups. These developments will shape whether Nvidia remains a leading force in the open‑AI ecosystem or repositions itself under new leadership.
