## New Privacy Promise
Ford has publicly committed to never sell customers’ driving data to third‑party data brokers. The statement was made during the launch of a new privacy‑focused website that lets owners adjust what they share.The promise comes in the wake of General Motors agreeing to pay $220 million to settle claims that it illegally sold driver and location information to data brokers.

## What the Promise Covers
Ford’s chief privacy officer, Kristin Jones, explained that the company will not sell data about how a vehicle is driven to brokers. The pledge is a direct response to the practice that some competitors, including Ford’s rival, had used to sell telematics to advertising, tracking, or analytics firms such as Adobe, LexisNexis, and Amplitude. Those firms gather vehicle and driving details to sell to insurance companies or target users with ads.

The promise does not eliminate all data sharing. Ford indicated that legally required exceptions remain, such as complying with police requests or working with outside service providers. Those agreements allow Ford to share data in limited circumstances that are mandated by law or contractual obligations.

## Customer Insight
To shape the new policy, Ford surveyed over 1,000 U.S. customers, both Ford and non‑Ford owners. The survey found that privacy is a top concern for most respondents. More than half of the participants also said they understand that some data sharing is acceptable and that they want control over what is shared. This balance between privacy and utility reflects the broader conversation about data use in connected vehicles.

## Trade‑offs of Opting Out
 However, disabling connected services can disable features such as BlueCruise, internet connectivity, vehicle location services in the Ford app, real‑time navigation, and built‑in roadside assistance.

## Broader Context
The promise arrives amid an ongoing feud between Ford and General Motors over data practices. While Ford’s statement explicitly avoids commenting on competitors’ actions, the timing of the new website launch—coinciding with the high‑profile GM settlement—has prompted some observers to see the move as a strategic differentiation. Nevertheless, Ford has not offered a detailed roadmap for how the privacy promise will be enforced or audited.

## Uncertainties and Limitations
Ford’s commitment to “never sell” driving data to brokers is clear, but the policy allows for data sharing under legal exceptions. Additionally, while the survey suggests that customers understand the value of data sharing, it does not quantify how many will actually opt out or how many features will be impacted by those choices.

## What to Watch Next
Consumers should monitor how the privacy settings perform in practice, especially regarding the availability and reliability of connected services. Ford’s future disclosures about compliance audits, transparency reports, or third‑party verification will also be important indicators of how seriously the promise is being upheld.

## Bottom Line
Ford’s new privacy promise marks a significant shift in how the automaker handles driver data. By publicly stating it will not sell driving behavior data to brokers, Ford distinguishes itself from competitors that have faced legal penalties for similar practices. The promise reflects growing consumer demand for privacy but also highlights the ongoing tension between data utility and personal control in connected vehicles.
