## Development of a wildlife‑aware edge system
 Sara Nozkova, who had spent 13 years in transportation and autonomous vehicles, noted how quickly road‑and‑rail systems fail when nature enters the equation. She spun out Flox Intelligence from KTH in 2020, and the company became a finalist at TechCrunch Disrupt’s Startup Battlefield 200 pitch competition in October 2026.
Flox’s devices use perception systems similar to those in autonomous vehicles, but their models are trained on wildlife behavior.Each unit is an edge-based physical AI that can operate 24/7 in harsh environments, where decisions have to be made in seconds. The company claims its technology can prevent collisions, damage and disruption across railways, airports, roads, agriculture and industrial sites, while supplying reporting through the Flox Wildlife Platform.

## Current deployments and funding
Nozkova reports about 150 Flox devices are live in North America, Europe and Australia. They sit at airfields and airport perimeters, road and rail corridors—especially fence ends and crossings along wildlife corridors—national parks, agricultural land, urban areas, mining sites and critical infrastructure such as electrical substations. The company also has OEM partnerships where clients pay an annual fee to deploy its software on vehicles, including trains.
In February 2026 Flox announced a €2.5 million Seed+ round to accelerate deployments across North America. The capital is intended to grow the system’s footprint and to build one of the world’s largest non‑lethal wildlife datasets, providing insights into how animals move and adapt alongside human infrastructure.

## What remains uncertain
While Flox claims its devices learn from each deployment, the evidence does not yet quantify effectiveness or long‑term behavioral change in wildlife.

## Takeaway
Flox Intelligence offers a novel, AI‑driven deterrent that differs from traditional sirens, lights, fences, falcons and culling.
