## The judgment and its background
On Wednesday, a New Mexico court ordered Meta, the parent of Facebook, to pay a $25 billion penalty for misleading consumers about data privacy. The award followed a jury finding that Meta had deceived users about a data breach involving a third‑party personality quiz that harvested data from roughly 87 million profiles and sold it to Cambridge Analytica, a political consulting firm. Judge Francis Mathew wrote that the jury’s findings of willful violations, the duration and repetition of the conduct, the breadth of subjects, the involvement of senior leadership, and the clear intentional and systematic nature of Meta’s repeated misstatements justified the highest level of penalty allowed under the New Mexico Unfair Practices Act—$5,000 per violation.

The case originated from the Cambridge Analytica scandal that erupted nearly a decade ago and shook the company, which had been led by Mark Zuckerberg. The scandal centered on allegations that Cambridge Analytica improperly accessed information from 87 million users, raising questions about Facebook’s data‑handling practices. The New Mexico suit is one of the first to bring that legacy of privacy concerns to a courtroom decision.
