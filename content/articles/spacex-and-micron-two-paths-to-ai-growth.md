## Investment Rationale
Investors often compare SpaceX (NASDAQ: SPCX) and Micron Technology (NASDAQ: MU) when considering AI exposure.SpaceX and Micron are two of the more popular artificial intelligence stocks among investors right now. SpaceX is pouring capital into AI infrastructure, targeting a total capacity of roughly 2 GW by the end of 2026 and near 10 GW by 2027. Micron, meanwhile, dominates the DRAM market, with 73 % of its last‑quarter revenue coming from DRAM and NAND, both of which remain in short supply.

## SpaceX’s AI Economics
SpaceX’s business model yields rapid returns on its AI spending. Within a year, short‑term leases of excess compute capacity generate revenue, and the company already has a $1.25 billion‑per‑month lease with Anthropic that will last through 2029, totaling $84.5 billion. These arrangements illustrate that SpaceX can monetize its AI build‑out quickly and at high margins.

## Micron’s Supply‑Side Advantage
Micron’s strength lies in its DRAM and NAND production. The current supply‑demand imbalance in DRAM is projected to persist through at least 2030, according to a rival, SK Hynix. As AI workloads grow, demand for high‑bandwidth memory—critical for GPUs—continues to rise, supporting sustained price pressure and revenue for Micron.

## Comparative Risks and Opportunities
While SpaceX’s aggressive expansion offers a clear path to a $1 trillion revenue target by 2030, it also exposes the company to capital‑intensive projects and lease commitments that could strain cash flows if market demand falters. Micron’s exposure to commodity‑like memory prices introduces a different risk profile: a prolonged supply squeeze could erode margins if technology shifts or new competitors enter the space.

## What to Watch
For SpaceX, monitoring the execution of its AI data‑center expansion and the performance of its lease agreements will be key. For Micron, tracking DRAM supply dynamics and the duration of the current shortage will help gauge future revenue stability.

## Takeaway
SpaceX’s rapid, lease‑backed AI build offers an attractive return pathway for investors willing to accept high capital intensity, whereas Micron’s DRAM dominance provides a more traditional, supply‑driven growth story.Both SpaceX and Micron are likely to see significant growth over the next few years.
