## Draft Proposal Targets Four‑Minute Finality
Ethereum researchers have filed a draft that would cut the wait for a transaction to become final from roughly 16 minutes to about four.  The change could allow money‑transfer services that hold customers’ payments while they clear to use their funds sooner.

The proposal was submitted by Ben Edgington, who coordinates Ethereum’s fast‑finality research, along with Francesco D’Amato, Roberto Saltini, Yann Vonlanthen and Mikhail Kalinin.

## How Finality Works and the Cost of Delay
In the current system, a transaction can enter an Ethereum block before the network treats it as final.Finality occurs after validators cast two full rounds of votes, after which a block is considered final.  Validators take turns casting votes; a block becomes final only after two full rounds of voting.  Because the process takes about 16 minutes, services that move funds off Ethereum must hold customers’ money for that duration.

The draft’s reduction to four minutes would lower the idle money cost from roughly $10,000 per transaction to about $67,000.  This figure reflects the amount tied up during the wait for finality.

## What’s Still Uncertain
The draft leaves several technical details unresolved, including voting timings, penalties, and parts of the security analysis.  Ben Edgington noted that many aspects remain to be determined, and the immediate aim is to obtain a proposal number to enter the formal upgrade pipeline.

While the proposal could make cross‑chain transfers cheaper, the exact impact will depend on how the final design is implemented and how quickly validators adopt the new rules.

## Takeaway
Ethereum’s fast‑finality draft proposes a fourfold reduction in finality time, which could significantly reduce the capital tied up during cross‑chain transfers.  The proposal is still under development, and key technical elements remain to be finalized before it can be adopted.
