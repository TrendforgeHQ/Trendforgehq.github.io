## New Taiwanese Regulation Targets AI‑Chip Smuggling
Taiwan announced a plan to criminalize the shipment of AI chips to China. The new rule would make such transfers a crime under the Foreign Trade Act, closing a legal loophole that has kept the activity technically legal. The change could reduce gray‑market demand that never appeared in official sales figures and lower regulatory risk for companies involved.

## China’s Potential Approval of Nvidia RTX PRO 5500
The Chinese government has signaled that it could allow domestic firms such as ByteDance and Alibaba to purchase Nvidia’s RTX PRO 5500, a chip built for high‑end professional computers. Industry executives expect the chip to sidestep existing U.S. export restrictions. Nvidia officials said that U.S. firms remain constrained by outdated export controls covering older gaming products and by China’s limits on U.S. imports. Alibaba and ByteDance have not yet commented.

## Implications for U.S. and Taiwanese Companies
The Taiwanese move would replace the existing controlled‑goods list with a blanket restriction covering the entire Chinese market.That would make these shipments a crime under the Foreign Trade Act. The change is part of a broader effort to align Taiwan’s trade controls with U.S. policy, following a trade agreement signed in January that reduced U.S. tariffs on Taiwanese goods. A legislative amendment adding a mainland China semiconductor clause is still pending.
