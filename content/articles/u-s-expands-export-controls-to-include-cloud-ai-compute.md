The U.S. House has passed the Remote Access Security Act, which adds cloud computing to the list of export‑controlled activities involving powerful AI chips from AMD and Nvidia. The bill requires firms to obtain licenses when they may provide compute to China or other restricted countries, closing a loophole that companies in China have used to access hardware.

## How the Act Tightens Control
The legislation extends export controls on potent AI chips to cloud computing, a practice that has previously allowed Chinese companies to obtain the hardware indirectly. The rule also imposes new licensing requirements for chip exports, reexports, or transfers to countries such as Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand, and Brazil. This expansion aims to prevent remote access to controlled technology.

## Why the Change Matters
U.S. lawmakers argue that closing these loopholes will strengthen national security and protect American innovation. The bill follows reports that a Shanghai‑based startup rented servers in Indonesia to acquire more than 2,300 banned Nvidia GPUs. The GPUs were allegedly purchased after the startup secured a Chinese client, raising concerns about how foreign firms may circumvent restrictions.

The act also addresses a specific case involving the Chinese company Megaspeed, which has reportedly been buying thousands of Nvidia chips to provide compute to Chinese businesses. The U.S. government has considered placing Megaspeed on a special entity list.

## Uncertainties and Next Steps
While the bill approves the export of Nvidia H200 chips to approved customers, China must still approve H200 imports before Chinese giants such as Alibaba and ByteDance can use them. The legislation’s effectiveness will depend on enforcement of the new licensing requirements and on whether foreign firms continue to use offshore rental arrangements.

Readers should watch for how the rule is implemented and whether additional countries are added to the list of restricted destinations.
